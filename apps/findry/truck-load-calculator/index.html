Estimate truck trips from quantity and truck capacity.

Findry’s Truck Load Calculator helps contractors and field crews estimate how many truck trips a material quantity may take. Enter the quantity, choose Volume or Weight, pick cubic yards or cubic metres when you work by volume, set a truck capacity, and Findry shows the load equivalent and the estimated number of trips. The Truck Load Calculator is part of the Findry app for iPhone and Android, and it is free for all users.

Step by step

How to use the Truck Load Calculator

Open Findry and tap Truck Load Calculator on the Home screen. A typical estimate takes four steps.

Step 1: Enter the material quantity

Type the amount of material you need to move. Use a quantity you already know from a supplier quote, a takeoff, or Findry’s Material Calculator.

Step 2: Choose Volume or Weight, then the unit

Pick Volume to work in cubic yards or cubic metres. A Weight option is also available in the app. Set the calculation type and unit before you finish the other fields, because changing them clears quantity and capacity.

Step 3: Set the truck capacity

Pick a preset from 5 to 20 cubic yards, enter a custom capacity for your own truck, or use cubic metres if that matches how you measure the vehicle.

Step 4: Calculate, then save or adjust

Tap Calculate to see the load equivalent by volume and the estimated number of trips. Use Edit Inputs to change a number, or save the result to Calculator History to reopen it later.

The formula

How to calculate truck loads and trips

The Truck Load Calculator uses the same math you would do by hand. Here it is in plain words, so you can check any estimate.

Load equivalent

Divide the material quantity by the truck’s capacity, using the same unit for both. For volume work: quantity (yd³) ÷ truck capacity (yd³) = load equivalent.

Estimated trips

Round the load equivalent up to a whole number of trips. A partly full truck still makes a trip, so 3.1 loads is 4 trips, and 16.67 loads is 17 trips.

Volume-based trips

When you work in volume units, trips are based on volume only, not on weight. Real trucks also have weight limits. Findry does not set legal payload or safe hauling capacity, so verify the vehicle and the load before you haul.

Example

Example: how many truck loads of fill dirt

(Illustrative example with made-up sample values.)

A contractor has 85 yd³ of fill dirt to haul and wants to compare two dump trucks.

  • With a 12 yd³ truck: 85 ÷ 12 = 7.08 loads, so 8 trips
  • With a 20 yd³ truck: 85 ÷ 20 = 4.25 loads, so 5 trips

Same quantity of fill dirt; the larger truck cuts the trip count from 8 to 5. If the quantity came from a pad or trench measurement, run it first in the Material Calculator, then use those cubic yards here.

Before you haul

Tips for planning truck trips

  • Match the unit to the quote. If the supplier sells by the cubic yard, work in cubic yards. If your takeoff is in cubic metres, switch the unit before you enter numbers.
  • Use the truck you will actually load. Presets cover common sizes from 5 to 20 yd³. For a different body size, enter a custom capacity instead of forcing a preset.
  • Round trips up, not down. A leftover 0.2 load still needs another run to the site or the dump.
  • Plan staging and turnaround. Trip count is only the hauling math. Access, dump time, and whether trucks can pass each other on site also affect the day.
  • Confirm weight and legal limits. Volume trips are not the same as a legal load. Moisture, material type, and the vehicle’s ratings can reduce how full you should load. Always verify before you haul.
  • Still need the yards? For gravel, dirt, sand, topsoil, and similar materials measured on site, start with the Material Calculator, then return here for trips.

FAQ

Truck load calculator FAQ

How do I calculate how many truck loads I need?

Divide the material quantity by the truck’s capacity, using the same unit for both, then round up to a whole number of trips. For example, 400 cubic yards ÷ 24 yd³ = 16.67 loads, which is 17 trips.

How many truck loads of gravel or dirt will I need?

Once you know the cubic yards of gravel, fill dirt, or another material, divide by your dump truck’s capacity in cubic yards and round up. Findry’s Truck Load Calculator does that math for you. If you still need the cubic yards from a length, width, and depth, use the Material Calculator first.

What dump truck capacities does Findry include?

For cubic yards, Findry includes presets of 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20 yd³, plus a custom capacity field for other sizes. You can also work in cubic metres.

Does the Truck Load Calculator use volume or weight?

You can choose Volume or Weight in the app. When you work in volume, you pick cubic yards or cubic metres, and trips are based on volume. Results are estimates and do not set legal payload or safe hauling limits.

Why does Findry round trips up?

A partly full truck still makes a trip. The load equivalent can be a decimal (for example 16.67 loads), and Estimated Trips is that number rounded up to a whole truck count.

Is the Truck Load Calculator free, and what phones does it work on?

Yes. The Truck Load Calculator and the Material Calculator are completely free for all users, on both the free plan and Findry Pro, and no subscription is needed. Findry is free to download for iPhone (iOS 16.4 or later) and Android (7.0 or later). You can save calculations to Calculator History and reopen them later.
