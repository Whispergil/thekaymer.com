Use this free trench backfill calculator to estimate how much gravel, sand, fill dirt, or other material you need to backfill a utility trench. The formula

How to calculate trench backfill

The calculator uses the same math you would do by hand. Here it is in plain words, so you can check any estimate.

1. Trench volume

Trench volume = length × width × depth. In Imperial, use feet for all three to get cubic feet. In Metric, use metres to get cubic metres.

2. Pipe volume

Pipe volume = π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × length, with π ≈ 3.1416. Convert the diameter to the same unit as the trench first: inches ÷ 12 for feet, or millimetres ÷ 1,000 for metres. Use the outside diameter of the pipe.

3. Backfill volume

Backfill = trench volume − pipe volume. Divide cubic feet by 27 to get cubic yards, because one cubic yard is 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft. One cubic metre is about 1.308 cubic yards.

4. Weight and trips

Estimated weight = cubic yards × the material’s weight per cubic yard. Load equivalent = backfill ÷ truck capacity, and trips is that number rounded up, because a partly full truck still makes a trip.

Example

Example: gravel backfill for a 100 ft water line trench

(Illustrative example with made-up sample values.)

A crew is backfilling a trench 100 ft long, 2 ft wide, and 4 ft deep around a 6-inch pipe with gravel, using a 10 yd³ truck.

  • Trench volume: 100 × 2 × 4 = 800 ft³ (29.63 yd³)
  • Pipe volume: 3.1416 × (0.5 ÷ 2)² × 100 = 19.63 ft³ (0.73 yd³)
  • Backfill: 800 − 19.63 = 780.37 ft³ ÷ 27 = 28.9 yd³ (22.1 m³)
  • Estimated weight: 28.9 × 1.4 = 40.46 US short tons of gravel
  • Loads: 28.9 ÷ 10 = 2.89 loads, so 3 trips

Materials

Material weights used

These are the same built-in estimates as the Findry Material Calculator, in US short tons (2,000 lb) per cubic yard.

  • Gravel: 1.4 tons per yd³
  • Crushed Stone: 1.5 tons per yd³
  • Sand: 1.35 tons per yd³
  • Topsoil: 1.1 tons per yd³
  • Fill Dirt: 1.2 tons per yd³
  • Mulch: 0.35 tons per yd³
  • Concrete: 2 tons per yd³
  • Asphalt Millings: 1.35 tons per yd³

Real weights vary by product, moisture, and supplier, so check with your supplier before you order by the ton.

Before you dig and order

Tips for trench backfill

  • Call 811 before you dig. Request a locate a few working days ahead so existing utilities are marked, and follow your state’s rules.
  • Measure the average depth. Trench depth often changes along the run. Measure in a few places and use the average, or split the run into sections.
  • Use the outside pipe diameter. The outside diameter gives a more accurate pipe volume than the nominal size.
  • Plan for compaction. The calculator does not add extra for compaction or waste. Ask your engineer or supplier how much more to order.
  • Check the spec for each zone. Bedding, haunch, and final backfill may need different materials. Run one calculation per zone if they differ.
  • Document before you backfill. Once the trench is closed, the pipe is hard to find again. Take photos and record depth, size, and reference points first. See how to document buried utilities.

FAQ

Trench backfill calculator FAQ

How do I calculate trench backfill?

Multiply the trench length × width × depth to get the trench volume. If a pipe goes in the trench, subtract the pipe volume: 3.1416 × (pipe diameter ÷ 2)² × length. In feet, divide the cubic feet by 27 to get cubic yards. For example, a 100 ft × 2 ft × 4 ft trench is 800 cubic feet. A 6-inch pipe takes up about 19.63 cubic feet, so the backfill is about 780.37 cubic feet, or 28.9 cubic yards.

How much gravel do I need for a trench?

Work out the backfill volume in cubic yards, then multiply by the weight of the gravel if your supplier sells by the ton. This calculator uses about 1.4 US short tons per cubic yard for gravel, the same estimate as the Findry Material Calculator. A 100 ft × 2 ft × 4 ft trench with a 6-inch pipe needs about 28.9 cubic yards, or roughly 40.46 tons of gravel. Confirm the weight with your supplier.

How much gravel do I need for a 100 ft trench?

It depends on the width and depth. A 100 ft trench that is 2 ft wide and 1 ft deep holds about 7.41 cubic yards, or roughly 10.37 tons of gravel. At 2 ft wide and 4 ft deep, it holds about 29.63 cubic yards before any pipe is subtracted. Enter your own measurements in the calculator above.

Should I subtract the pipe from the backfill?

Yes, if you want the volume of material that goes around and over the pipe. Small pipes barely change the total, but large pipes and culverts can take up a lot of the trench. Enter the outside diameter of the pipe for the best estimate.

Does the calculator include compaction or bedding?

No. It uses exactly the measurements you enter and does not add a waste, swell, or compaction allowance. Backfill is usually placed and compacted in lifts, and the bedding, haunch, and cover zones may call for different materials. Ask your engineer or supplier how much extra to order and which material each zone needs.

How many truck loads will I need to backfill a trench?

Divide the backfill volume by your truck’s capacity, using the same unit, and round up to whole trips. For example, 28.9 cubic yards ÷ a 10 yd³ truck = 2.89 loads, so 3 trips. Trips are based on volume only, not legal payload or weight limits.

Do I need to call 811 before I dig a trench?

Yes. In the United States, call 811 or submit a request online a few working days before you dig, so buried utilities can be marked. Local rules vary, so check the requirements in your state. Findry does not detect or locate underground utilities.
