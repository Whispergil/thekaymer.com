Step by step

How to use the Material Calculator

Open Findry and tap Material Calculator on the Home screen. A typical estimate takes five steps.

Step 1: Choose the material

Pick Gravel, Crushed Stone, Sand, Topsoil, Fill Dirt, Mulch, Concrete, or Asphalt Millings. The material you choose sets the estimated weight.

Step 2: Choose a truck capacity

Work in cubic yards or cubic metres. For cubic yards, pick a common truck size from 5 to 20 yd³, or enter a custom capacity for your own truck, such as 24 yd³.

Step 3: Pick Imperial or Metric

Choose your measurement units before you type anything. Changing units clears the measurements you have already entered.

Step 4: Enter length, width, and depth

In Imperial, enter length and width in feet and depth in inches. Measure the area you need to fill or cover, and use the depth of the finished layer.

Step 5: Calculate, then save or adjust

Tap Calculate to see cubic yards, cubic metres, estimated US short tons, the load equivalent, and the estimated number of trips. Use Edit Measurements to change a number, or save the result to Calculator History to reopen it later.

The formula

How to calculate cubic yards and tons of material

The Material Calculator uses the same math you would do by hand. Here it is in plain words, so you can check any estimate.

Volume in cubic yards

Multiply length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft) to get cubic feet. Depth is usually measured in inches, so divide inches by 12 first. Then divide cubic feet by 27, because one cubic yard is 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft = 27 cubic feet.

Cubic metres

One cubic yard is about 0.765 cubic metres, so Findry also shows the volume in m³.

Estimated weight in tons

Findry applies a built-in weight for the material you selected and shows the result in US short tons (2,000 lb). Gravel, sand, topsoil, mulch, and the other materials all weigh different amounts, and real weights vary by product and moisture. Check with your supplier before you order by the ton.

Truck loads and trips

The load equivalent is the volume divided by your truck’s capacity. Trips is that number rounded up to whole trucks, because a partly full truck still makes a trip. Trips are based on volume only, not on weight.

Example

Example: crushed stone for a 150 ft × 55 ft lot

(Illustrative example using the same numbers as the app screenshot on this page.)

A crew needs a 3-inch layer of crushed stone over an area 150 ft long and 55 ft wide, using a 20 yd³ truck.

  • Cubic feet: 150 × 55 × (3 ÷ 12) = 2,062.5 ft³
  • Cubic yards: 2,062.5 ÷ 27 = 76.39 yd³
  • Cubic metres: about 58.4 m³
  • Estimated weight: the app estimates about 114.6 tons for crushed stone
  • Loads: 76.39 ÷ 20 = 3.82 loads, so 4 trips

Example

Example: gravel for an 80 ft × 40 ft pad

(Illustrative example with made-up sample values.)

A contractor is placing a 4-inch layer of gravel on an 80 ft × 40 ft pad and has a 10 yd³ truck.

  • Cubic feet: 80 × 40 × (4 ÷ 12) = 1,066.67 ft³
  • Cubic yards: 1,066.67 ÷ 27 = 39.51 yd³
  • Cubic metres: about 30.2 m³
  • Loads: 39.51 ÷ 10 = 3.95 loads, so 4 trips
  • Weight: select Gravel in Findry to see the estimated tons, and confirm the weight with your supplier.
  • Custom truck: with a 24 yd³ truck entered as a custom capacity, the same pad is 39.51 ÷ 24 = 1.65 loads, so 2 trips.

Before you order

Tips before you order gravel, soil, or fill

  • Measure the finished depth. Use the depth of the layer after it is placed and compacted, not the loose pile.
  • Break odd shapes into rectangles. Run one calculation per rectangle, save each to Calculator History, and add the yards together.
  • Plan for extra. The calculator uses exactly the measurements you enter and doesn’t add a waste or compaction allowance. Fill dirt, topsoil, and base materials often settle, so ask your supplier or engineer how much extra to order for your job.
  • Ask how it’s sold. Some suppliers sell by the cubic yard and others by the ton. Findry shows both, so you can compare against the quote.
  • Confirm weight and truck limits. Trips are based on volume. Legal hauling limits depend on the vehicle and the load weight, so check them before you rely on an estimate.

FAQ

Material calculator FAQ

How do I calculate cubic yards of material?

Multiply length in feet by width in feet by depth in feet, then divide by 27. If your depth is in inches, divide it by 12 first. For example, 150 ft × 55 ft × 3 in is 150 × 55 × 0.25 = 2,062.5 cubic feet, or about 76.39 cubic yards.

How much gravel, sand, or topsoil do I need?

Measure the length, width, and finished depth of the area and work out the cubic yards. Then convert to tons if your supplier sells by weight. Findry’s Material Calculator does both for gravel, crushed stone, sand, topsoil, fill dirt, mulch, concrete, and asphalt millings. Confirm the final quantity with your supplier.

How does Findry estimate tons of material?

Findry applies a built-in weight for the material you choose to the calculated volume and shows the result in US short tons. It is an estimate. Actual weight depends on the exact product, moisture, and supplier, so check with your supplier before ordering by the ton.

How many truck loads will I need?

Divide the cubic yards by your truck’s capacity in cubic yards, then round up to a whole number of trips. In Findry you can pick a truck size from 5 to 20 cubic yards or enter a custom capacity, and it shows both the load equivalent and the estimated trips. Results are estimates and do not set legal payload or safe hauling limits.

Does the calculator add extra for waste or compaction?

No. The Material Calculator uses exactly the measurements you enter. If your job needs extra material for waste, compaction, or uneven ground, ask your supplier or engineer how much to add.

Is the Material Calculator free, and what phones does it work on?

Yes. The Material Calculator and the Truck Load Calculator are completely free for all users, on both the free plan and Findry Pro, and no subscription is needed. Findry is free to download for iPhone (iOS 16.4 or later) and Android (7.0 or later). You can save calculations to Calculator History and reopen them later.
