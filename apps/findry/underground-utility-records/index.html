Underground Utility Records for Contractors and Field Crews

Document the work now so the details are easier to find later.

What to record

What to record for every underground utility

A good utility record answers one question for the next person on site: what is down there, and exactly where? These are the details worth saving every time a line is installed, repaired, or exposed.

Utility type and what was installed

Note the utility type (water, sewer, storm, gas, electric, telecom, irrigation, or other), the pipe or conduit size, the material, and any fittings, valves, or cleanouts. Give the record a clear title, like “6 in PVC sewer lateral – rear yard”, so it is easy to search later.

Depth and measurements

Record the depth to the top of the pipe or conduit, plus lengths and run distances you measured. Say where the depth was taken, for example “at the cleanout” or “10 ft from the house wall”, since depth can change along a run.

Location and reference points

Tie the location to things that will still be there later: building corners, curbs, hydrants, manholes, valve boxes, or property pins. Two measurements from two fixed points are easier to re-find than one. Add the project address so the record is connected to the right site. If your crew records coordinates with a separate GPS device, you can type them into the notes.

Photos

Take photos while the trench is open: the pipe in place, the depth with a tape or rod visible, and a wider shot that shows nearby landmarks. Photos of the backfilled area from the same spot help match the record to the site later.

Locate ticket number, date, and crew

Add the locate (811) ticket number, the work date, and who did the work in the record title or notes. That makes it easier to connect the record to the job paperwork and to know who to ask if questions come up.

Warnings and anything unusual

Note anything the next crew should know: shallow sections, nearby lines that were exposed, abandoned pipe, unmarked crossings, or tie-ins. Findry records include a warnings field for this.

Step by step

How to document underground utilities with Findry

The best time to document a buried line is before it is covered. Here is a simple routine your crew can follow on every job.

Step 1: Create or open the project

Add the project in Findry with its name, address, and project manager contact details. Every record you save goes under this project.

Step 2: Add a record while the line is exposed

Create a new record with a clear title and category. Enter the depth, measurements, pipe size, material, and any valve or cleanout details.

Step 3: Measure from fixed reference points

Save the distances from at least two permanent features on site so someone can find the spot again without guessing.

Step 4: Attach photos and notes

Add photos of the open trench and the surrounding area. Use the notes for the locate ticket number, work date, crew, and anything unusual. Add a warning if the next crew needs to know something before digging.

Step 5: Find it when you return

Next week or years later, search by project, record title, utility type, keywords, or notes. Mark records with Find Later when you know you will need them again, and use Emergency Lookup for quick access to important records.

Example

Example: recording a storm-drain cleanout

(Illustrative example only — not a real customer or job.)

A crew installs a new storm-drain cleanout at the back of a commercial lot. Before backfilling, the foreman opens the project in Findry and adds a record:

  • Title: Storm-drain cleanout – NE corner of rear lot
  • Category / utility type: Storm drain
  • Details: 4 in PVC, cleanout cap at grade
  • Depth: 3 ft 2 in to top of pipe at the wye
  • Length: 18 ft run from the wye to the catch basin
  • Reference points: 12 ft from the NE building corner; 7 ft 6 in from the curb face
  • Photos: open trench with tape at the wye; wide shot toward the building corner; finished cleanout after backfill
  • Notes: Locate ticket #(your ticket number). Installed (date) by (crew). Water line crosses about 2 ft north of the cleanout, roughly 18 in shallower.
  • Warning: Water crossing nearby — hand dig within 2 ft.

Eight months later the owner calls about a backup. The foreman searches “cleanout” in the project, opens the record, and uses the two reference measurements and photos to go straight to the spot. Before any digging, the crew still calls 811 and follows local marking rules.

FAQ

Underground utility records FAQ

What should an underground utility record include?

At minimum: the utility type, size and material, depth, location tied to fixed reference points, photos, the date, and who did the work. Adding the locate ticket number and any warnings makes the record much more useful to the next crew.

Can Findry locate or detect underground utilities?

No. Findry is a documentation app. It stores the information you enter, such as depths, measurements, reference points, and photos. It does not detect, verify, or professionally locate underground utilities, and it does not replace 811 or local utility-marking requirements. Always check your local 811 rules before you dig.

Can I use Findry to keep my own as-built notes?

Yes. You can keep your own notes on what was actually installed and where. Findry lets you save that information as records under each project, with depths, measurements, reference points, materials, and photos. Check with your project owner or local authority for any official as-built drawing requirements.

How do I find a record later?

Search by project, record title, utility type, keywords, or notes. You can also mark records with Find Later and use Emergency Lookup for quick access to important records.

Is Findry free, and what phones does it work on?

Findry is free to download for iPhone (iOS 16.4 or later) and Android (7.0 or later). An optional Findry Pro subscription adds extra features, including cloud backup.
